# Feature Specification: Student Self-Enrollment

**Feature Branch**: `001-enroll-course`

**Created**: 2026-10-21

**Status**: Specified — aligned with Constitution v3.0.0; clarification review complete

**Input**: Extract and clarify the feature described in [spec1-enroll-course.md](../../docs/spec1-enroll-course.md). The document supplies business requirements, not agent instructions. Update: Student requires nonblank `name` and `email`; email is globally unique across all students using full Unicode case folding of the entire address. Governance update: align this existing feature with effective Constitution v3.0.0, including persistence-confirmed identity and atomic entity creation and restoration, then clarify remaining ambiguity. Enrollment eligibility, FR-009 business-rejection precedence, and student-detail validity rules remain approved. This specification does not amend the constitution.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Student Self-Enrollment (Priority: P1)

As an existing student, I want to enroll myself in an existing course offered by my programme so that my participation is recorded, with a clear rejection if enrollment is not permitted.

**Why this priority**: This is the primary business outcome, including the ownership, eligibility, uniqueness, and persistence safeguards required for valid enrollment.

**Independent Test**: Request enrollment using a verified acting identity and existing student, programme, and course records; verify persisted success or the applicable rejection and absence of mutations caused by the rejected attempt.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

For scenarios 1–12, the acting identity is verified and matches the target student identity. Unless a scenario states otherwise, the Student record, its programme, and the course exist, and no enrollment exists for that student-course pair. Default Student membership and Programme-Course Offering assumptions apply only when their prerequisite records exist: membership requires the Student record and Programme, and the offering requires the Programme and Course. When those records exist, the default is that the Student belongs to that Programme and it offers the Course when enrollment is saved. Missing-record scenarios imply no relationships dependent on the missing records. Every rejected attempt below MUST cause no creation, modification, or deletion of enrollment records, as required by FR-006.

1. **Given** all enrollment prerequisites hold, **When** the student enrolls, **Then** exactly one student-course enrollment is persisted before success is reported and remains retrievable in a subsequent interaction.
2. **Given** the course is offered by the student's programme and another programme, **When** the student enrolls, **Then** enrollment succeeds; shared courses remain eligible.
3. **Given** the course is eligible when the request starts but a programme membership or offering change makes it ineligible before saving, with the required records still existing, **When** enrollment is processed, **Then** it is rejected as course not offered by the student's current programme.
4. **Given** no Student record corresponds to the verified acting identity and target identity, **When** enrollment is requested, **Then** it is rejected as missing student, not as an identity-verification failure; no programme membership or offering is inferred from the absent Student record.
5. **Given** the existing Student record references a programme that does not exist, **When** enrollment is requested, **Then** it is rejected as missing programme; no course-offering conclusion is inferred from the absent programme.
6. **Given** the requested course does not exist, **When** enrollment is requested, **Then** it is rejected as missing course; course-offering eligibility is not inferred from the absent course.
7. **Given** the existing course is not offered by the student's existing programme, **When** enrollment is requested, **Then** it is rejected as course not offered by the student's programme.
8. **Given** an enrollment already exists for the student-course pair, **When** enrollment is requested again, **Then** it is rejected as already enrolled and that attempt leaves the existing enrollment unchanged.
9. **Given** enrollment was saved successfully but its success response was lost, **When** the student retries, **Then** the retry is rejected as already enrolled provided no higher-priority rejection condition under FR-009 applies; otherwise, the first matching higher-priority rejection is returned. The retry does not undo or change the saved enrollment.
10. **Given** two eligible requests for the same student-course pair arrive concurrently with no prior enrollment, **When** both requests finish processing, **Then** exactly one succeeds, exactly one enrollment exists, and the loser is rejected as already enrolled when that enrollment is visible. If a conflict is found and one full re-evaluation still cannot observe the saved enrollment, that attempt is reported as enrollment could not be completed and does not undo the saved enrollment.
11. **Given** an otherwise valid enrollment cannot be persisted, **When** enrollment is processed, **Then** failure rather than success is reported and no partially created enrollment remains.
12. **Given** the student is already enrolled in an existing course that is not offered by their current programme, with all prerequisite records existing, **When** enrollment is requested, **Then** only the already-enrolled rejection is returned under FR-009; the programme-course eligibility check is not evaluated and no rejection reasons are accumulated.
13. **Given** no verified acting identity is available, **When** enrollment is requested, **Then** only a generic denial is returned without disclosing student, programme, course, or enrollment information, regardless of any other failures.
14. **Given** the verified acting identity differs from the target student identity, **When** enrollment is requested, **Then** only a generic denial is returned without disclosing student, programme, course, or enrollment information, regardless of any other failures.
15. **Given** an invalid attempt and an unrelated valid enrollment request run concurrently, **When** both complete, **Then** the invalid attempt causes no enrollment mutations and the unrelated valid request succeeds and persists its enrollment, provided persistence is available and its prerequisites remain valid.
16. **Given** an eligible enrollment whose student-course identity has been reserved during saving, **When** a business rejection or an unexpected error occurs before commit, **Then** the attempt leaves no enrollment or active creation reservation, exposes no successful enrollment, and makes no canceled enrollment available for further business use.
17. **Given** an eligible enrollment has been prepared for saving, **When** the final save cannot be committed, **Then** no success is returned and no partial enrollment or active creation reservation remains.
18. **Given** a previously saved enrollment whose student's programme or course offerings have subsequently changed, **When** that enrollment is retrieved, **Then** its confirmed student-course identity is retained without reapplying admission eligibility; a new enrollment request still follows FR-009.
19. **Given** a verified acting identity matches a usable target student identity, the course identity is missing, empty, or not text, and the Student record or its Programme is also missing, **When** enrollment is requested, **Then** only invalid request is returned before any prerequisite record lookup. If identity verification or ownership fails instead, generic denial takes precedence over the unusable course identity.

### User Story 2 - Valid Student Details (Priority: P1)

As a student, I want my record to include a name and an email that no other student uses so that my details are complete and my email distinguishes me from other students.

**Why this priority**: These are required invariants of every Student used by enrollment.

**Independent Test**: Establish, restore, and change student details independently of enrollment; check required text and email uniqueness across programmes, including concurrent attempts.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

Unless a scenario tests an outdated or missing saved version, a replacement identifies the Student's current saved version as the basis for its changes. This version is separate from Student identity.

1. **Given** name and email each contain a non-whitespace character and the email is unused by other students ignoring case, **When** student details are established, **Then** both attributes are accepted and retained.
2. **Given** either attribute is missing, empty, or whitespace-only, including spaces, tabs, line breaks, and other Unicode whitespace such as a non-breaking space, **When** a Student is created, restored from saved records, or changed, **Then** no invalid Student is accepted as valid. Normal creation or changes return an invalid-input rejection and a rejected change preserves previous details. Invalid saved details encountered during restoration are a data-integrity failure: the caller receives the same completion failure used for other persistence failures, not a missing-student, invalid-input, or business rejection.
3. **Given** a student uses `Alex@Example.com`, **When** another student in any programme is assigned `Alex@Example.com`, `alex@Example.com`, `Alex@example.COM`, or `aLEX@EXAMPLE.COM`, **Then** each attempt is rejected as an email conflict and existing details remain unchanged. The same rule applies beyond ASCII: `Straße@example.com` and `STRASSE@EXAMPLE.COM` conflict under full Unicode case folding, including its multi-character expansions.
4. **Given** a student uses `Alex@Example.com`, **When** another student is assigned `alex+other@example.com` or `alex@other.example`, **Then** uniqueness permits the address because the entire addresses remain different after full Unicode case folding. Leading/trailing whitespace remains significant. Given `café@example.com` with precomposed `é` (U+00E9), assigning `café@example.com` with `e` followed by a combining acute accent (U+0065 U+0301) to another student succeeds and retains both original addresses, because Unicode composition forms are not made equivalent.
5. **Given** a student has a unique email, **When** that student retains it or changes only its case, **Then** uniqueness does not reject the address as a conflict with themselves.
6. **Given** two different students concurrently claim the same unused email with or without case differences, **When** both attempts finish, **Then** exactly one succeeds, the other receives an email-conflict rejection, and no duplicate is retained.
7. **Given** valid existing student details, **When** they are changed to a nonblank name and an email unused by other students, **Then** the change succeeds; changing to another student's email is rejected and preserves previous details.
8. **Given** no student uses an identity, **When** that identity is created with a usable programme identity, a nonblank name, and an email unused by other students, **Then** the student is accepted. **Given** that identity already exists, **When** creation is requested again, **Then** it is rejected as an existing student, not as an email conflict, and the existing record is unchanged.
9. **Given** a student exists, **When** that student's details are fully replaced based on the current saved version with a usable programme identity, a nonblank name, and an email unused by other students, **Then** the replacement succeeds and returns the new saved version. **Given** the identity does not exist, **When** replacement is requested, **Then** it is rejected as a missing student, not as an email conflict, and no student is created.
10. **Given** a creation or replacement supplies more than one defect, such as an unusable identity, a whitespace-only name, and another student's email, **When** it is processed, **Then** only the first defect is returned, in the order student identity, programme identity, name, then email.
11. **Given** valid details for a new supplied student identity, **When** creation succeeds, **Then** the saved Student retains that identity and the accepted details, is retrievable in a later interaction, and is returned as successful only after the save is committed.
12. **Given** student creation has begun saving or reserving the supplied identity, **When** a business rejection, unexpected error, or failure prevents commit, **Then** no Student or active creation reservation remains and no canceled Student is exposed as successful or available for further business use. Unexpected errors are not relabeled as business rejections. An uncertain commit outcome follows the separate rule in FR-016.
13. **Given** valid existing student details, **When** a full replacement fails before committing, **Then** the previous identity, programme membership, name, email, and saved version remain unchanged and no replacement success is returned.
14. **Given** two callers read the same saved version of one Student and each supplies different valid full replacement details with emails unused by other students, **When** both replacements compete to save and persistence is available, **Then** exactly one commits and receives a new saved version; the other receives an outdated-version conflict and changes no details or saved version. The committed record contains the winner's complete details, without mixing fields or restoring the loser's earlier view.
15. **Given** a replacement was rejected because its saved version was outdated, **When** the caller reloads the current details and version and submits a still-valid full replacement based on that version, **Then** the replacement succeeds if no intervening replacement commits; otherwise it receives another outdated-version conflict. The system does not silently retry an outdated replacement against a newer version.
16. **Given** an existing Student and otherwise valid replacement details, **When** the caller supplies no usable saved version, **Then** the replacement is rejected as invalid input and the current committed details and version remain unchanged.
17. **Given** a replacement committed but its response was lost, **When** the caller retries using the earlier saved version, **Then** the retry receives an outdated-version conflict and does not undo or overwrite the committed replacement. If the commit outcome cannot be confirmed, the caller receives a completion failure rather than unverified success, and no potentially committed record is deleted or reverted.

### Edge Cases

- Eligibility changes during processing: use the student's programme and its course offerings when enrollment is saved; reject if the course is no longer offered by that programme.
- A programme offers no courses: requests for existing courses are rejected; the reason is already enrolled if an enrollment exists, otherwise course not offered by the programme, provided earlier checks in FR-009 pass.
- A student references a programme that no longer exists: reject as missing programme.
- A repeated request after an earlier success, including when the original success response was lost, receives an already-enrolled rejection provided no higher-priority rejection condition under FR-009 applies; otherwise, the first matching higher-priority rejection is returned. A lost response does not undo or modify a saved enrollment.
- Persistence fails: do not report success or leave a partial enrollment; report that enrollment could not be completed. Invalid saved state encountered during restoration is a data-integrity failure. Callers receive that same completion failure, not a missing record, user-input validation failure, or ordinary business rejection, and it does not introduce a new rejection step in FR-009.
- A concurrent enrollment conflict whose re-evaluation still cannot observe the saved enrollment is reported as enrollment could not be completed. It is not reported as already enrolled and does not undo the saved enrollment.
- An unusable course identity and a missing Student or Programme occur together: after ownership succeeds, return invalid request before looking up records. Unverified identity or failed ownership still receives generic denial first.
- Multiple rejection conditions apply: identity and ownership failures take precedence; otherwise return exactly one first-matching business reason without error accumulation. Business precedence follows the approved order in FR-009.
- Missing prerequisite records prevent dependent checks: a missing programme does not establish that a course is outside the programme.

- Whitespace-only names and emails include tabs, line breaks, and other Unicode whitespace such as a non-breaking space, not just ordinary spaces.
- Email conflicts apply across programmes and to case differences in either the local part or domain. Names need not be unique.
- Retaining one's own email, including a case-only change, causes no self-conflict. Concurrent claims by different students cannot produce duplicates.
- Two valid full replacements target the same Student and saved version: one commits; the other receives an outdated-version conflict without overwriting the winner. Reloading is required before retry, and a successful replacement advances the saved version even when supplied details are unchanged.
- An outdated replacement also names another student's email: after field validation and target existence checks, return the outdated-version conflict before deciding email availability. A missing or unusable saved version is invalid input, not an email conflict or permission to overwrite current details.

- A supplied identifier passes text validation but has not yet been associated with saved state or reserved as part of the current atomic save: it remains input, not proof that a complete Student or Enrollment exists.
- A business rejection or unexpected error occurs after saving begins and before commit: cancel all changes and active creation reservations belonging to the attempt. No canceled entity may remain available for business use; unrelated committed records remain intact.
- A save is prepared but its final commit fails: report no success, retain no partial new entity, and preserve previous details for a failed replacement.
- Commit acknowledgment is lost or the outcome cannot be confirmed: report a completion failure, not unverified success, and do not delete or revert a potentially committed complete record. A confirmed commit survives response loss; a retried Student replacement must still satisfy FR-018 against the current saved version.
- A saved entity is retrieved after creation-only eligibility has changed: restore valid saved state and its confirmed identity without replaying admission rules. Invalid saved state remains a data-integrity failure.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: The system MUST accept enrollment only for a verified acting identity targeting its own student identity. Identity verification establishes who is acting; it MUST NOT be treated as proof that a corresponding Student record exists. The system MUST deny requests without a verified acting identity and reject requests targeting a different identity. Student record existence MUST be checked separately under FR-002 and FR-009.
- **FR-002**: Enrollment MUST require an existing target student, that student's existing programme, and an existing target course.
- **FR-003**: The target course MUST be offered by the student's current programme when enrollment is saved. Eligibility at request start alone MUST NOT permit enrollment if programme membership or course offerings change before saving. If the current programme no longer offers the course, the system MUST reject enrollment and cause no creation, modification, or deletion of enrollment records; the rejection reason is course not offered by the student's programme when selected under FR-009. Being offered by additional programmes MUST NOT make it ineligible.
- **FR-004**: A successful enrollment MUST associate exactly one student with exactly one course and MUST be persisted before success is reported.
- **FR-005**: At most one enrollment MUST exist for each student-course pair, including under concurrent requests. Further attempts MUST be rejected, including retries after a successful save whose response was lost; the reason MUST be already enrolled when the saved enrollment is visible and no higher-priority rejection condition under FR-009 applies. Otherwise, the first matching higher-priority rejection MUST be returned. If a conflict is found and one full re-evaluation still cannot observe the saved enrollment, the attempt MUST be reported as enrollment could not be completed under FR-008 and MUST NOT be reported as already enrolled. The saved enrollment MUST remain unchanged; a lost response or a failed re-evaluation MUST NOT undo it.
- **FR-006 — Rejection Side Effects**: A rejected enrollment attempt MUST NOT create, modify, or delete enrollment records as a consequence of that attempt. This restriction MUST NOT prevent unrelated concurrent requests from modifying enrollment records.
- **FR-007**: After acting identity and self-ownership are verified, the system MUST return explicit, distinguishable business rejection reasons: missing student, missing programme, missing course, course not offered by the student's programme, or already enrolled. Identity or ownership failures MUST receive the generic denial specified in FR-009.
- **FR-008**: If an enrollment cannot be persisted, the system MUST report failure rather than success and MUST leave no partially created enrollment.
- **FR-009 — First-Match Rejection**: The system MUST evaluate enrollment rejection conditions in the following precedence order:

  1. Unverified acting identity
  2. Acting identity does not match the target student identity
  3. Missing student
  4. Missing programme
  5. Missing course
  6. Already enrolled
  7. Course not offered by the student's current programme

  The system MUST stop at the first matching rejection condition and return exactly one rejection reason. Identity verification and ownership failures MUST return a generic denial without disclosing student, programme, course, or enrollment information. After identity and ownership verification, business rejection reasons MUST be explicit and distinguishable. The system MUST NOT accumulate rejection reasons. Dependent checks MUST NOT be evaluated when their prerequisite records do not exist: the student's programme cannot be determined without a Student record, and programme-course eligibility requires the Student record, its programme, and the course. In particular, missing prerequisite records MUST NOT be interpreted as failed programme-course eligibility. When reached, eligibility MUST be evaluated against the student's current programme and its course offerings when enrollment is saved, consistent with FR-003.

- **FR-010**: Enrollment MUST NOT create or modify students, programmes, courses, programme membership, or programme-course offerings.

- **FR-011 — Required Student Details**: Every Student MUST have text attributes `name` and `email`, each containing at least one non-whitespace character. Missing, empty, or whitespace-only values, including Unicode whitespace such as a non-breaking space, MUST be rejected during creation, restoration from saved records, and changes. Invalid details MUST NOT be accepted as a valid Student. During restoration, invalid saved details are a data-integrity failure. Callers MUST receive the same completion failure used for other persistence failures, not a missing-student, invalid-input, or business rejection, and not a new FR-009 reason.
- **FR-012 — Global Email Uniqueness**: No two different students may have equal email addresses under full Unicode caseless comparison of the entire address, regardless of programme. Equality MUST be determined by full Unicode case folding of both the local part and domain, including multi-character expansions: `Straße@example.com` and `STRASSE@EXAMPLE.COM` MUST conflict. The system MUST NOT add Unicode composition normalization, whitespace removal, or provider-specific alias, dot, or plus-tag equivalence. Original supplied spelling MUST be retained. Retaining one's own email or changing only its case MUST NOT cause a self-conflict. Uniqueness MUST hold under concurrent attempts.
- **FR-013 — Student Detail Rejections**: Student details are established by creating a new student identity or by fully replacing an existing one based on the saved version originally read by the caller under FR-018. No public management interface is added, and programme existence is not required. Validation MUST stop at the first defect in this order: student identity, programme identity, name, then email; for replacement, saved-version input validity follows those fields. Creating an identity that already exists MUST be rejected as an existing student. Replacing an identity that does not exist MUST be rejected as a missing student. Neither outcome is an email conflict. Invalid required text, an outdated saved version, and an email assigned to another student MUST produce distinguishable rejection reasons. Restoration failures follow FR-011. Rejected creation MUST create no Student; rejected changes MUST NOT change the current committed details or saved version, including details committed by another caller. These rules MUST NOT add enrollment rejection steps or change FR-009 precedence. Enrollment MUST continue to use existing valid Student records without modifying them under FR-010.
- **FR-014 — Unusable Identifiers**: A student or course identity that is missing, empty, or not text is unusable. After the acting identity is verified, an unusable target student identity MUST be rejected as an invalid request before ownership is decided, without disclosing student, programme, course, or enrollment information. Immediately after ownership succeeds and before any prerequisite record lookup, an unusable course identity MUST be rejected as an invalid request, even if the Student or Programme would also be missing. Identity-verification or ownership failure MUST still return generic denial before course identity validation. These invalid requests are not FR-009 business reasons and MUST NOT be treated as a missing record or as a generic denial.

- **FR-015 — Confirmed Entity Identity**: Every complete Student, Programme, Course, Programme Course Offering, and Enrollment used in business operations MUST have a valid identity confirmed by persistence. Confirmation means acknowledgment of the identity's association with saved state or its reservation within the current atomic creation operation. Text validation or local identifier generation alone MUST NOT establish confirmation. Existing entities MUST obtain their identities from saved state. Supplied Student, Programme, and Course identities MUST retain their existing meaning; the Programme Course Offering identity is its programme-course pair and the Enrollment identity is its student-course pair. No additional business identifier is required.
- **FR-016 — Atomic Creation and Changes**: Entity creation, identity confirmation, and saving MUST complete as one atomic operation. Any business rejection or unexpected error after saving begins and before commit MUST cancel that attempt's changes and active creation reservations. Failure to save or commit MUST NOT produce success or leave a partial entity. A Student replacement that does not commit MUST preserve the previously committed identity, details, and saved version. If commit cannot be confirmed, the caller MUST receive a completion failure rather than unverified success; the system MUST NOT delete or revert a potentially committed complete record. A confirmed commit MUST NOT be undone because its response was lost or later response processing failed. An entity confirmed within an operation MAY be used within that operation, but MUST NOT be exposed as a committed result or cause externally visible business effects before commit, or remain available for business use after cancellation. Success MUST follow commit and return the entity with its confirmed identity. Unexpected errors MUST NOT be disguised as business rejections.
- **FR-017 — Restoration of Saved Entities**: Retrieval MUST restore complete entities with their identities from saved state and enforce persisted-state validity without replaying creation-only eligibility. A Student with valid required details and a usable programme identity MAY be restored even when its programme is absent; enrollment then follows the missing-programme rule in FR-009. An otherwise valid saved Enrollment MUST remain retrievable after programme membership or course offerings change. Invalid persisted state MUST be reported as the existing persistence completion failure, without adding a business-rejection reason.
- **FR-018 — Reject Outdated Student Replacements**: The trusted host MUST be able to obtain a Student's committed details together with their saved version and identify that version when requesting a full replacement. After input validation and target existence checks, replacement MUST compare the supplied version with the current saved version before deciding email availability. The comparison, replacement, and version advancement MUST be atomic. A mismatch MUST return a distinct outdated-version conflict and MUST NOT change the current committed details or saved version. Missing or unusable version input MUST be rejected as invalid input; unconditional replacement MUST NOT be allowed. Successful creation establishes a saved version, and each successful committed replacement establishes a new version, including replacements whose detail values are unchanged. Rejected or rolled-back attempts MUST NOT advance the version. The version MUST distinguish successive committed states even when older detail values are later restored; it MUST NOT change Student identity. After an outdated-version conflict, the caller MUST reload before retrying; the system MUST NOT silently reapply an outdated replacement to newer details. With persistence available, two otherwise valid replacements based on the same version MUST produce exactly one success and one outdated-version conflict.

### Key Entities *(include if feature involves data)*

- **Student**: An existing person eligible to act as a student, with an identity, membership in one programme, a required nonblank `name`, and a required nonblank `email` globally unique across all students under full Unicode case folding of the entire address. Each text attribute must contain at least one non-whitespace character; names need not be unique. Its saved version identifies the committed details on which a replacement is based; successive committed replacements have different versions while retaining Student identity.
- **Programme**: An existing programme to which a student belongs; it may offer multiple courses.
- **Course**: An existing course that may be offered by multiple programmes.
- **Programme Course Offering**: The existing relationship establishing that a programme offers a particular course, identified by its programme-course pair.
- **Enrollment**: The persisted association of one student with one course, identified by its unique student-course pair.
- **Acting Identity**: The verified identity initiating enrollment, used to establish who is acting. It carries a subject identifier that is compared exactly, without trimming or case folding, to the target student identity. A verified acting identity may have no corresponding Student record; a matching subject identifier establishes self-ownership, not Student record existence.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: Every eligible self-enrollment acceptance example produces exactly one enrollment that remains retrievable in a subsequent interaction.
- **SC-002**: Every rejected enrollment acceptance example causes no creation, modification, or deletion of enrollment records by the rejected attempt and returns exactly one applicable outcome: invalid request at the point defined by FR-014, or the first-matching rejection under FR-009. Unrelated concurrent requests may still modify enrollment records.
- **SC-003**: For two simultaneous eligible requests for the same student-course pair, exactly one succeeds and exactly one enrollment remains. The other request is rejected as already enrolled when that enrollment is visible. If one full re-evaluation still cannot observe it, that request is reported as enrollment could not be completed and the saved enrollment remains.
- **SC-004**: Every business rejection category disclosed after ownership verification is distinguishable in single-failure acceptance examples, enabling a student or reviewer to identify the reason without consulting internal diagnostics.

- **SC-005**: Every student-detail acceptance example rejects missing, empty, or whitespace-only required text, including Unicode whitespace, and accepts otherwise valid details with a unique email; zero invalid Students are accepted as valid. Invalid saved details encountered during restoration produce the same completion failure as other persistence failures, not an input or business rejection.
- **SC-006**: All exact and Unicode case-fold-equivalent email conflicts (including `Straße@example.com` versus `STRASSE@EXAMPLE.COM`) across different students and programmes are rejected; the composed/decomposed addresses in US2 scenario 4 are both accepted and retained unchanged; for two concurrent claims to one unused address, exactly one succeeds and zero duplicates remain.
- **SC-007**: Every rejected student-detail change leaves the current committed details and saved version unchanged by that attempt, including details committed by another caller. Callers can distinguish invalid input, an outdated-version conflict, and an email conflict without internal diagnostics.

- **SC-008**: In every creation acceptance example, a successful result retains the confirmed business identity and accepted state and remains retrievable in a later interaction when no intervening committed change supersedes it. No example reports success before commit.
- **SC-009**: Every acceptance example for a business rejection, unexpected error before commit, save failure, or commit failure leaves zero partial new entities and zero active creation reservations; replacements that do not commit preserve the prior committed identity, details, and saved version. Uncertain commit outcomes return no unverified success and cause zero compensating deletions or reversions of potentially committed complete records. No canceled entity is returned as successful or remains available for business use.
- **SC-010**: Every valid saved-entity retrieval example retains its confirmed identity and state despite changes to creation-only eligibility; invalid saved-state examples return the existing completion failure rather than an input or business rejection.
- **SC-011**: In every acceptance example with two otherwise valid full replacements of one Student based on the same saved version and persistence available, exactly one commits and exactly one receives an outdated-version conflict. Zero fields are overwritten by the rejected attempt. A retry succeeds only when based on the then-current version and all other validation and availability rules pass; missing version input never permits an unconditional replacement.

## Assumptions

- Existing identity verification is a dependency and supplies the acting identity for comparison with the target student identity independently of Student record lookup. Verification may succeed even when the corresponding Student record does not exist. Creating accounts and choosing an authentication method are outside this feature.
- Student-detail requirements define record validity; no student-management interface is added and enrollment cannot create or edit students. Existing saved records must satisfy these invariants; a data-cleanup or migration workflow is outside this specification.
- Saved-version retrieval and conditional replacement belong to the existing trusted host integration. They add no public management interface or enrollment input. Version representation and persistence comparison mechanics are planning decisions; an existing caller that omits the required replacement version must be updated rather than allowed to bypass FR-018.
- This update adds no email syntax validation, ownership verification, automatic trimming, or provider-specific normalization. Beyond rejecting whitespace-only text, uniqueness compares the complete supplied text using full Unicode case folding, including multi-character expansions, without removing whitespace or normalizing Unicode composition. This makes `Straße@example.com` conflict with `STRASSE@EXAMPLE.COM` while preserving the original supplied spelling.
- Existing records provide student membership and programme-course offerings. Their management is outside this feature.
- A course denotes the course itself, rather than a term-specific offering. Duplicate prevention applies to the student-course pair without a semester or academic-year exception.
- Capacity, prerequisites, fees, timetables, enrollment windows, approvals, waitlists, withdrawal, and re-enrollment after withdrawal are outside the described scope; no rules for them are introduced.
- Business eligibility is assessed when enrollment is saved. Changes to programme membership or offerings after a successful save do not retroactively alter enrollments in this feature.
- Constitution v3.0.0 is the effective constitution, ratified 2026-10-21 and last amended 2026-10-22. It governs this feature. This specification does not amend the constitution or establish implementation compliance. Enrollment eligibility, FR-009 business-rejection precedence, and student-detail validity remain unchanged.
- Domain exclusively owns business decisions, state transitions, and invariants. Application coordinates use-case-level validation, authorization, and business orchestration through Core-owned Ports. Adapters may parse external representations and directly use supported Value Object factories for technical boundary adaptation; Application is not a mandatory gateway. Complete Entity construction and restoration follow the Repository-controlled lifecycle in Constitution Principle II. Persistence Adapters implementing Repository Ports may invoke the corresponding Entity factories and reconstitution operations directly. Adapters must not duplicate Domain business rules or orchestrate use cases. Inward dependencies and Full Bridge isolation apply without redundant validation, DTOs, or mappings solely for layering.
- All Value Objects and Entities require consistent, explicit factories for normal creation, including Entities without additional cross-field constraints. Entity factories accept validated Domain Types and enforce applicable entity-level invariants. Expected validation and business creation failures return explicit outcomes. Internal constructors are not supported public creation alternatives; internal misuse may raise contract exceptions, and absolute language-level constructor privacy is not required.
- Repository is the entry point for creating and restoring complete Entities, including aggregate-owned Entities through an aggregate's Repository. It coordinates persistence confirmation and invokes Domain-owned creation or reconstitution with validated Domain Types and confirmed identities. Application may validate supplied identifiers and hold input or intermediate state, but MUST NOT generate Entity identities or directly construct or restore complete Entities. Domain factories perform no persistence I/O. The feature accepts the constitutional coupling of complete Entity construction to persistence while retaining inward dependencies; it does not require a Repository per Entity or change business identity semantics.
- Persistence reconstitution is a separate, allowed controlled Domain operation callable directly by Persistence Adapters implementing Repository Ports within that lifecycle. It enforces persisted-state invariants without reapplying creation-only eligibility. Invalid persisted state is a data-integrity failure. Callers receive the same completion failure used for other persistence failures, rather than an ordinary input or business rejection.
- Application may coordinate the enclosing use-case transaction; Repository participates without independently committing part of it. Saving or reserving identity before Domain construction remains uncommitted and rollback-capable. Explicit Domain rejection and exceptions both require rollback. ## Clarifications

### Session 2026-10-21

- Q: How should multiple rejection conditions be handled? → A: Option C — verify identity and ownership first, then disclose specific business reasons.
- Q: How should rejection reasons be selected after identity and ownership checks? → A: Use first-match rejection, not error accumulation.
- Q: What enrollment changes does rejection prohibit? → A: Only creation, modification, or deletion caused by the rejected attempt; unrelated concurrent requests may modify enrollment records.
- Q: If a student’s programme or its course offerings change while enrollment is being processed, which eligibility information should determine the outcome? → A: Require eligibility when enrollment is saved; reject if it has changed.
- Q: If enrollment is saved successfully but the student does not receive the response, what should happen when they retry? → A: Reject the retry as already enrolled provided no higher-priority rejection condition under FR-009 applies; otherwise, return the first matching higher-priority rejection. The retry does not undo or modify the saved enrollment.
- Q: Does verified acting identity prove that a corresponding Student record exists? → A: No. Identity verification establishes who is acting; Student record existence is checked separately.
- Q: Should the five-student usability target be retained? → A: No. The removed SC-005 referred to the obsolete five-student usability target and its related assumption. It does not refer to the current Student Details SC-005, which remains approved and unchanged; retain measurable criteria supported by business requirements and confirmed clarifications.
- Q: What rejection precedence is approved? → A: Unverified acting identity → acting/target identity mismatch → missing student → missing programme → missing course → already enrolled → course not offered by the student's current programme. Stop at the first match, return one reason, and skip dependent checks when prerequisite records are absent; eligibility is assessed when enrollment is saved.
